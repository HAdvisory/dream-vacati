Mexico Travel Guide

Cancún, Tulum, Playa del Carmen, Mexico City — which part of Mexico fits your trip? Entry requirements, safety advisories by state, and regional planning guide.

Updated November 2026 • North America • Complete Planning Hub

Choose Your Region Riviera Maya Mexico City Other Regions Best Time Entry Requirements Safety Advisories Budget Toolkit FAQs

Mexico is not one destination — it is a country with more variation in climate, culture, infrastructure, and travel conditions than many people realize when booking a trip. The same passport that gets you into Cancún's hotel zone also takes you to Mexico City's world-class museums, the Pacific surf coast of Oaxaca, and the colonial architecture of Guadalajara. The safety picture, weather window, transportation logic, and travel style all differ significantly across these regions.

This guide helps you choose which part of Mexico matches your trip goals, understand what entry looks like for US citizens, and plan around the state-by-state safety picture. Choose Your Region

The most useful first question for planning a Mexico trip is not "where should I stay" — it is "what kind of trip am I taking?" Mexico's main traveler destinations separate roughly into three types: beach resort destinations on the Caribbean coast, beach and surf destinations on the Pacific coast, and city/cultural destinations. These require different planning approaches, different timing, and have different safety and logistics profiles.

The Riviera Maya: Cancún, Playa del Carmen, and Tulum

The Riviera Maya is the stretch of Caribbean coastline south of Cancún down to Tulum, encompassing approximately 130 kilometers of beaches, cenotes (freshwater sinkholes), Mayan ruins, and dive sites. It is the most visited part of Mexico by international tourists and has three distinct urban centers with meaningfully different characters.

Sargassum on the Riviera Maya

The Caribbean coast of Mexico experiences annual sargassum (seaweed) influxes that have been increasingly significant since 2015. The worst months are typically late April through September. Cancún's Hotel Zone has the most aggressive seaweed removal programs; some beaches further south, including parts of the Tulum coastline, can experience heavier accumulation. If a pristine beach experience is a primary trip priority, check current sargassum conditions closer to your travel date through travel forums and hotel social media accounts. This is a separate planning consideration from weather and hurricanes.

Mexico City (CDMX)

Mexico City is one of the world's great city travel destinations — a 21-million-person metropolitan area with world-class museums (the Anthropology Museum alone is worth a dedicated day), a food scene that has earned repeated recognition on global rankings, an extraordinary collection of pre-Columbian and colonial architecture, and neighborhoods ranging from the historic Zócalo to the design-forward galleries and restaurants of Roma and Condesa.

The US State Department rates Mexico City at Level 2 — Exercise Increased Caution as of May 2026, citing crime. Violent and non-violent crime occur throughout the city; petty theft is common in both tourist and non-tourist areas. The neighborhoods popular with travelers — Polanco, Roma, Condesa, Coyoacán, the Historic Center — are generally manageable with standard urban precautions. Avoid ostentatious displays of valuables, use registered taxis or Uber rather than hailing on the street, and follow local guidance on which areas to explore at night.

Getting to Mexico City

Mexico City has two operational airports. Benito Juárez International Airport (MEX) is the original airport, located 5 km east of the Zócalo in the city's east. It is the busiest and best-connected of the two — served by American, Delta, United, Alaska, Aeroméxico, and multiple international carriers. Felipe Ángeles International Airport (AIFA/NLU) opened in March 2022 and is located approximately 35 km north of central Mexico City in Zumpango, State of Mexico — a substantially longer transfer requiring a car or bus rather than the metro; allow 60–90 minutes for the ground transfer to central Mexico City. AIFA serves primarily budget Mexican carriers (VivaAerobus, Volaris). Travelers booking cheap tickets should verify their specific carrier and terminal before travel and confirm which airport their flight arrives at before assuming a short transfer to the city center.

Other Mexican Regions

Oaxaca

Oaxaca occupies a unique position in Mexican travel — the city of Oaxaca de Juárez is widely considered to have the finest regional cuisine in Mexico (mole, tlayudas, mezcal, chocolate), and the surrounding valleys contain some of the country's most significant pre-Columbian archaeological sites including Monte Albán. The Pacific coast reaches — Puerto Escondido and Huatulco — are surf and beach destinations accessible from the city with some planning. The US State Department rates Oaxaca at Level 2; the main tourist areas in Oaxaca City and the coastal destinations are explicitly cleared with no travel restrictions for US government employees. Direct flights from the US are limited; most routes connect through Mexico City (MEX).

Guadalajara and Puerto Vallarta (Jalisco)

Jalisco is rated Level 3 — Reconsider Travel by the US State Department as of May 2026, citing crime, terrorism, and kidnapping. This is a meaningfully different safety classification than Quintana Roo or Mexico City. The city of Guadalajara and the beach resort of Puerto Vallarta are accessible and have tourism infrastructure, but the broader state has significant cartel-related security concerns — particularly on highway routes through rural Jalisco. US government employees face specific travel restrictions on two highway routes within the state. If you are considering Guadalajara or Puerto Vallarta, review the full state advisory and understand that the Level 3 classification reflects state-wide conditions, not just the urban areas. Source: US State Department, May 2026.

When to Visit Mexico

Mexico's optimal travel windows differ significantly by region. There is no single "best time to visit Mexico" — the right answer depends on where you are going.

Riviera Maya Best
December – April
Dry season; outside hurricane season; warm, low humidity. Peak prices.
Riviera Maya Shoulder
May & November
Lower prices; some rain possible; still good beach weather most days.
Riviera Maya Risk
August – October
Atlantic hurricane peak. Travel insurance with cancellation coverage essential.

Mexico City is year-round viable for city travel. The dry season runs November through April; the rainy season (May–October) brings afternoon and evening rains but rarely disrupts a full day's sightseeing. The city sits at 2,240 meters altitude — temperatures are mild year-round, rarely exceeding 25°C (77°F) in the warmer months. Altitude adjustment can affect visitors in the first day or two; stay hydrated and avoid heavy exertion immediately on arrival.

Oaxaca City has an agreeable climate year-round. The dry season aligns with the broader Mexican winter (November–April). July through August brings more rain to the valleys but is also the season for the celebrated Guelaguetza cultural festival (late July).

Hurricane season: The Atlantic hurricane season (June 1 – November 30, statistical peak around September 10) affects Quintana Roo, the Yucatán Peninsula, and the Gulf coast, including Cancún, Tulum, and Playa del Carmen. The Eastern Pacific hurricane season (May 15 – November 30) is a separate basin and can affect Mexico's Pacific coast, including Puerto Vallarta and the Oaxaca coastline. If you are visiting both coasts on the same trip, the relevant risk window differs by region. Source: NOAA National Hurricane Center — Tropical Cyclone Climatology, verified November 2026.

Entry Requirements for US Citizens

Verify before you travel: Entry requirements can change. Check the US State Department and official Mexican consular guidance for current requirements before your trip.

Visa

US citizens and legal permanent residents do not need a visa to enter Mexico for tourism. No advance visa application is required.

Passport validity

Your passport must be valid for the duration of your stay in Mexico. Mexico does not require a minimum 6-month validity beyond arrival — your passport simply cannot expire while you are in the country. Note: US airlines may apply their own validity requirements; confirm with your carrier if your passport is close to expiry.

Tourist card / FMM — current status

The paper Forma Migratoria Múltiple (FMM) tourist card was replaced with a digital immigration process beginning July 28, 2022. For air arrivals at major Mexican airports including Cancún (CUN), Mexico City (MEX), and Guadalajara (GDL), no paper form is needed — your information is captured electronically and you receive a stamp in your passport on arrival that records your authorized period of stay. The authorized stay duration is determined by the immigration officer at entry; travelers may receive anywhere from 30 to 180 days at the officer's discretion. The number stamped in your passport is your authorized period — do not assume a full 180 days.

Land borders and sea arrivals: Paper FMM forms may still be required at some land border crossings and sea ports of entry. If you are driving into Mexico or arriving on a cruise, you may receive or need to complete a paper form. The applicable visitor fee (Derecho de No Residente / DNR) for land and sea arrivals is approximately 983 MXN per 2026 official fee schedules (Instituto Nacional de Migración / 2026 Héroes Paisanos guide). Note: under Mexico's Federal Law of Rights (Article 11, Section II(a)), a DNR fee exemption applies to qualifying land-entry visitors who remain in Mexico for no more than 7 days — the statute does not impose a northern-border geographic restriction. If the stay exceeds 7 days, the applicable DNR fee (983 MXN in 2026) becomes payable upon departure. Not every land-entry visitor automatically qualifies; travelers should confirm their eligibility at the port of entry or with INM before assuming the exemption applies. The exemption does not eliminate the requirement to comply with applicable immigration procedures and obtain the appropriate immigration record, and immigration officials determine the authorized stay duration at entry. Verify requirements at your specific crossing point before travel. Source: guidance from Instituto Nacional de Migración (INM) and official Mexican consular advisories.

Air arrival fees: No separate FMM fee is charged for air arrivals under the digital transition. However, fees and processes at land and sea entry points continue to vary by location — do not assume a universal fee elimination applies at all entry points.

Safety Advisories by State

The US State Department issues state-by-state travel advisories for Mexico using a 1–4 scale. The advisories below cover the states most commonly visited by US tourists. The full list is available at travel.state.gov. Source: US State Department, updated May 2026.

Oaxaca

Jalisco

Multiple Mexican states are Level 4 — Do Not Travel, including Colima, Guerrero, Michoacán, Sinaloa, Tamaulipas, and Zacatecas. These are not tourist destinations for most travelers. If your trip involves ground transportation through any state, verify the advisory level for each state you transit. Source: US State Department, May 2026.

Budget Planning for Mexico

Mexico's cost of travel varies as much as its geography. The Riviera Maya all-inclusive corridor is comparable in price to the Dominican Republic or Jamaica. Playa del Carmen and Mexico City can be significantly cheaper for independent travelers. Tulum runs higher than most travelers expect given its "off the beaten path" aesthetic.

These are illustrative planning scenarios, not verified market averages. Actual prices vary by travel dates, booking platform, resort tier, and availability. All figures are in USD unless otherwise noted and reflect per-person estimates for double-occupancy unless stated.

  • Cancún Hotel Zone (all-inclusive): $180–400 per person per day (illustrative range), depending on resort tier. Excludes international flights.
  • Playa del Carmen (independent): $80–160 per person per day (illustrative range) for mid-range accommodation, local restaurants, and activities. One of the best value options in the Riviera Maya for independent travelers.
  • Tulum: $200–450+ per person per day (illustrative range) on the beach road; $80–140 per person per day (illustrative range) in Tulum downtown. The gap between beach-road boutique hotel pricing and downtown is unusually wide.
  • Mexico City: $70–150 per person per day (illustrative range) for a solid mid-range city experience — excellent-value restaurants, manageable accommodation, world-class free and low-cost museums and cultural sites.

Roundtrip flights from the US to Cancún (CUN) typically run $200–400 outside peak season from the East Coast (illustrative; verify with airlines for current fares); Mexico City (MEX) is similar. Flights to Oaxaca (OAX) or Guadalajara (GDL) from the US East Coast often require a connection and can run $350–550 roundtrip (illustrative; verify with airlines for current fares).

Frequently Asked Questions About Mexico

Do US citizens need a visa to visit Mexico? +
No. US citizens and legal permanent residents enter Mexico visa-free for tourism. No visa application or paper FMM tourist card is required for air arrivals at major airports — Mexico replaced the paper FMM with a digital process beginning July 2022. You receive a passport stamp on arrival. The authorized stay duration is determined by the immigration officer at entry; it may range from 30 to 180 days and is recorded in your passport stamp — do not assume a guaranteed minimum period. For air arrivals, the visitor fee (Derecho de No Residente / DNR) is typically incorporated into the airfare and not separately charged at the airport. For land and sea arrivals, the 2026 fee per official INM schedules is approximately 983 MXN. Is Mexico safe for tourists in 2026? +
Safety varies significantly by state. Quintana Roo (Cancún, Playa del Carmen, Tulum, Cozumel) is Level 2 — Exercise Increased Caution. Mexico City is Level 2. Oaxaca is Level 2. Jalisco (Guadalajara, Puerto Vallarta) is Level 3 — Reconsider Travel. Several other states are Level 4 — Do Not Travel. Research the specific state advisory at travel.state.gov before booking any Mexican destination. Source: US State Department, May 2026.
Should I visit Cancún, Playa del Carmen, or Tulum? +
Cancún is best for first-time Riviera Maya visitors who want all-inclusive resort convenience, wide cleaned beaches, and easy airport logistics (20 min from CUN). Playa del Carmen is best for budget-conscious travelers who want walkability, local restaurants, Cozumel ferry access, and good value. Tulum suits couples and wellness travelers but has higher costs and more complicated logistics — Tulum Airport (TQO) opened December 2023 but route availability from US cities has been limited and subject to change; verify current service before planning to use TQO. Most Tulum visitors still route through Cancún CUN. Read the full Cancún guide for Hotel Zone planning details.
What is the best time to visit Mexico? +
For the Riviera Maya (Cancún, Playa, Tulum), December through April is the best window — dry, sunny, and outside hurricane season. November is a good shoulder month. The Atlantic hurricane season (June through November, peak August–October) affects the Caribbean coast. For Mexico City, the city is year-round viable — dry season is November–April. Oaxaca City is also year-round; July–August brings rain but also the Guelaguetza festival.
