Cancún Travel Guide

Hotel Zone vs downtown, beaches, resort selection, CUN airport logistics, and what first-time visitors get wrong before they book.

Updated November 2026 • Mexico • Caribbean Coast

Overview Hotel Zone Beaches Airport & Getting There Resort Selection Budget Couples & Families Best Time Common Mistakes Toolkit FAQs

Cancún is the most visited destination in Mexico for a reason. The Hotel Zone sits on a narrow barrier island shaped like a number 7, with Caribbean-blue water on one side and the calmer Nichupté Lagoon on the other. The beaches in the northern stretch of the Hotel Zone rank consistently among the Caribbean's best — white powder sand, clear turquoise water, minimal surf. The resort infrastructure is mature, the airport handles direct flights from dozens of US cities, and the all-inclusive model is well-established enough that pricing is predictable and competitive.

What catches first-time visitors off guard is the Hotel Zone's size and layout. The strip is 14 miles long. A resort at the northern end is not interchangeable with one at the southern end — they have different beach quality, different proximity to the airport, and different access to the downtown bus terminal. Choosing the wrong end for your trip goals is one of the most common and avoidable mistakes. This guide covers all of it.

Part of the Mexico hub: This guide covers Cancún specifically. For coverage of Playa del Carmen, Tulum, Mexico City, regional safety advisories, and entry requirements for US citizens, see the Mexico Travel Guide.

What Kind of Destination Is Cancún?

Cancún is primarily an all-inclusive beach resort destination. The Hotel Zone has more than 100 large resorts ranging from budget-tier properties to luxury mega-resorts. For travelers whose main goals are beach time, pool access, and simplicity — the meals, drinks, and entertainment are covered and you don't need to arrange much once you arrive — Cancún delivers that model reliably.

It is not the right choice for travelers who want to explore local markets daily, eat at independent local restaurants, or avoid being in a heavily touristed zone. It is also not the best choice if your primary interest is cenote access, colonial architecture, or deeper Mexican culture — Playa del Carmen and the surrounding area gives you better access to those elements while still being within range of the Caribbean coast.

Cancún's strengths: mature all-inclusive market with competitive pricing, direct flights from most major US cities, some of the best Caribbean beaches on the continent, good organized excursion infrastructure (Chichen Itza, Tulum ruins, Isla Mujeres, cenotes are all commonly visited day trips from Cancún), and predictable logistics.

The Hotel Zone: North, Middle, and South

The Hotel Zone (Zona Hotelera) is not a single uniform strip — the character, beach quality, and logistics differ meaningfully between its three sections. Knowing which section your resort is in matters more than many travelers realize when booking.

The R-1 bus runs the full length of the Hotel Zone and continues into downtown Cancún. The fare is approximate — verify the current rate posted on vehicles, as fares can be adjusted. It is the most cost-effective way to move along the strip. Taxis within the Hotel Zone are fixed-rate by zone and are generally used for resort-to-resort or strip-to-airport trips.

Downtown Cancún (El Centro)

Downtown Cancún is the working city behind the Hotel Zone — a grid-plan urban area with local markets (Mercado 23 and Mercado 28 are the primary ones), cheaper restaurants, the main ADO bus terminal (buses to Playa del Carmen, Tulum, and Chichen Itza depart from here), and budget accommodation options. Most Hotel Zone resort guests have no reason to visit downtown unless they are using the bus terminal for a day trip or are specifically looking for local food options. It is not a cultural destination in the way that Playa del Carmen or Mexico City are. That said, Mercado 23 is a legitimate local market worth visiting if you want to see a Cancún that exists outside the resort corridor.

Beach Guide

The Hotel Zone's northern beaches — roughly Km 3 to Km 8 — are consistently ranked among the best in Mexico and the broader Caribbean. The sand is white and powdery, the water is an unusually vivid shade of turquoise due to the shallow shelf, and the Caribbean northeast exposure keeps wave activity minimal in calm weather. This is the stretch where the most famous Hotel Zone photos originate.

Key Beaches by Location

  • Playa Delfines (Km 17.5): The most photographed public beach in Cancún — the large "CANCUN" sign is here. It faces the open Caribbean with more surf than the northern beaches. No resort backing it; free public access with a large parking lot. Views of the full Hotel Zone strip from here are excellent. Worth a visit even if staying in the north.
  • Playa Chac Mool (Km 10): Popular public beach near the Forum shopping mall. More wave action than the north; popular with body boarders and younger crowds. Street food vendors nearby.
  • Playa Tortuga (Km 7): Public beach with calm, shallow water. Watersports rentals and beach clubs available. Good for families with children.
  • Playa Las Perlas (Km 2.5): One of the calmest and least crowded public beaches on the strip. Shallow, clear water. No major services but very relaxed atmosphere.
  • Isla Mujeres beaches: The ferry to Isla Mujeres departs from Puerto Juárez (near the north end of the Hotel Zone). Playa Norte on Isla Mujeres is a consistently top-ranked Caribbean beach — calm, shallow, and less crowded than the Hotel Zone on most days. The 20-minute ferry crossing is straightforward and inexpensive. Worth a day trip from any Hotel Zone resort.

Sargassum seaweed affects Hotel Zone beaches variably from April through September, with some years significantly worse than others. Resorts in the northern Hotel Zone and on Isla Mujeres tend to fare better than southern stretches. Check current sargassum reports closer to your travel dates — the annual extent is not predictable far in advance. Source: Sargassum monitoring programs updated seasonally.

Beach Flag System

Mexican beaches use a colored flag system managed by SEMAR (the Mexican Navy). These flags are posted at hotels and public beaches and are enforced — you will be asked to leave the water under a red flag. The flags are:

  • Blue: Optimal conditions — calm water, low current, no hazards
  • Yellow: Moderate caution — waves, mild current; swim carefully
  • Red: Danger — strong currents, high waves, or hazards; no swimming
  • Black/Purple: Extreme conditions or jellyfish/marine hazard present; stay out of the water

The flag system applies to all public beaches and is taken seriously. Drownings in Cancún are almost always associated with ignoring red flag conditions.

CUN Airport and Arrival Logistics

Cancún International Airport (IATA: CUN) is one of Mexico's busiest and best-connected airports. Direct flights operate from most major US hubs including New York (JFK, EWR), Miami (MIA), Atlanta (ATL), Dallas (DFW), Chicago (ORD), Houston (IAH), Los Angeles (LAX), and many others. American, Delta, United, Southwest, JetBlue, Spirit, Frontier, and several charter carriers all serve CUN from the US. Flight time from Miami is about 1 hour 45 minutes; from New York about 3 hours 30 minutes; from Dallas about 2 hours 45 minutes.

CUN has four terminals (T1–T4). Most major US carriers use T2, T3, or T4. T4 is the newest and handles most international arrivals. Terminal assignments change — confirm your terminal before arrival via your airline.

Getting from CUN to the Hotel Zone

  • Official taxi kiosks (recommended): Posted fixed-rate taxi fares are located in the arrivals hall before you exit the secure zone. Pay here and get a voucher. Fares are fixed by zone — verify the current rate at the official kiosk on arrival. As an illustrative planning figure, Hotel Zone transfers have typically run approximately $35–55 USD depending on your hotel's zone location (southern vs northern Hotel Zone), but the posted kiosk rate is the authoritative price. Do not pay more than the posted rate and do not negotiate with drivers who approach you before the kiosk.
  • Pre-booked shuttles: Shared shuttles (collectivos) are cheaper than taxis for groups of 2–4 and can be booked through your resort or reputable transfer companies before arrival. Expect $15–25 USD per person for a shared shuttle to the Hotel Zone.
  • ADO bus: ADO operates an airport bus service to downtown Cancún's main bus terminal for about $7–9 USD per person. From downtown you can take a taxi or the R-1 bus to the Hotel Zone. This is the cheapest option but adds 45–75 minutes total travel time and is not practical for families with luggage. Useful for budget travelers.
  • Ride-share apps: Uber and similar apps operate from CUN but are not permitted to pick up at certain terminals. Some travelers use them from the second floor departure level where restrictions are less enforced. This is a gray area — check current status before relying on it.

Timeshare solicitors operate heavily in the CUN arrivals area. You will be approached by people in official-looking uniforms offering "free" resort tours, free transportation, or complimentary breakfasts. These are timeshare pitches. Politely decline and proceed directly to the official taxi or shuttle kiosk. Do not accept "free" transportation offers from the terminal floor — they are not free.

Choosing a Resort

Cancún's resort market is well-developed, with clear tiers and reliable third-party review data available on major booking platforms. The key variables to evaluate when choosing a resort are beach access (is it directly on the beach or across the street?), adult-only vs family-friendly policies, food and bar quality, and Hotel Zone location relative to your interests.

When reading resort reviews, pay particular attention to recent reviews (within 6 months of your travel date) and look specifically for mentions of food quality, beach cleanliness, and check-in experience. All-inclusive resorts renovate frequently and the experience can shift significantly year to year. A resort with strong 2023 reviews may have changed management or undergone renovation in 2025.

Couples vs Families in Cancún

For Couples

Cancún is well-suited for couples at most resort tiers. The northern Hotel Zone has a high concentration of adults-only properties, which tend to have quieter pools, better cocktail programs, and an atmosphere more conducive to relaxation. Adults-only properties in Cancún range from boutique mid-range to full luxury. If you are traveling as a couple who values quiet beach time, an adults-only resort in the Km 3–8 stretch offers the most reliable experience. The nightlife in the Km 9 zone (Coco Bongo and surrounding venues) is accessible via taxi if that is part of your trip goals, but most couples staying in the north choose not to go.

For Families with Children

Cancún is one of the most established family all-inclusive destinations in the Caribbean. Large family-friendly resorts in the middle and north Hotel Zone have dedicated kids' clubs, waterpark features, shallow pool areas, and children's entertainment programs. Some northern Hotel Zone beaches tend to have shallower, calmer water that may be more suitable for young children, but actual swimming conditions depend on the specific beach, weather, surf, currents, and flag warnings on the day — always check the beach flag before entering the water. The R-1 bus is free for young children on a parent's lap. Most major resorts offer kids' clubs with programming from ages 4–12 at no extra cost as part of the all-inclusive. Confirm age ranges and hours before booking if this is a priority.

Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30. The statistical peak is around September 10. Cancún has taken direct hits from major hurricanes — Wilma (2005) and Gilbert (1988) are the most significant. Modern resorts are built to current hurricane standards and evacuation procedures are established, but travel insurance covering weather-related cancellation is strongly recommended for travel during June–November. Source: NOAA National Hurricane Center.

Spring Break Timing

US spring break falls primarily in mid-March through early April, with peak weeks varying by university calendar. During peak spring break weeks, Hotel Zone resort pricing increases significantly, beaches and pool areas are at maximum capacity, and the Km 9 nightlife district is extremely busy. If your trip goals are relaxation and beach time, avoid these weeks. If you are traveling with college-age travelers, this period has the most energy on the strip. The northern Hotel Zone tends to be less affected than the middle zone nightlife district.

Common Planning Mistakes

  1. Booking without checking which end of the Hotel Zone your resort is on. A resort at Km 20 is a 20-minute taxi ride from a resort at Km 5. The beach quality differs, the airport distance differs, and the access to key landmarks differs. Look up the kilometer marker of any resort you are considering before booking.
  2. Not confirming beach-direct access. Some Hotel Zone "beachfront" resorts are across a road from the beach, with an underpass or bridge to access it. For families with children or travelers for whom direct beach access is a priority, confirm before booking rather than after arrival.
  3. Accepting airport transportation from anyone other than the official kiosk. The CUN arrivals area has persistent solicitors offering transportation. The official fixed-rate kiosk is located inside the terminal before you exit. Using it is the only way to guarantee the posted rate and an official vehicle.
  4. Not accounting for excursion costs when budgeting. The all-inclusive rate covers meals and drinks at the resort. Chichen Itza, cenotes, Isla Mujeres, and Cozumel are common day trips — at $50–130 per person each, a family of four doing two excursions adds $400–1,000 that the all-inclusive rate does not cover. Build this into your total trip budget.
  5. Underestimating sargassum impact in summer. Beach photos from December–February do not represent what some Hotel Zone beaches look like in July–September. If your trip dates fall in summer months, check recent traveler reports of sargassum conditions at your specific resort section. The northern Hotel Zone and Isla Mujeres tend to be less affected.
  6. Booking non-refundable during hurricane season without travel insurance. If a named storm affects Cancún during your stay, the resort will typically accommodate you if you are already there — but if the storm hits before or during your travel dates and you have a non-refundable booking without travel insurance, your recourse is limited. This is especially important for travel in September and October.

Frequently Asked Questions About Cancún

What is the difference between the Cancún Hotel Zone and downtown Cancún? +
The Hotel Zone (Zona Hotelera) is a 14-mile barrier island strip with the vast majority of large all-inclusive resorts, the best Caribbean-facing beaches, the main shopping malls, and most tourist infrastructure. Downtown Cancún (Ciudad Cancún or El Centro) is a grid-plan city on the mainland — cheaper hotels and restaurants, local markets, the main bus terminal, and where most of the workforce lives. Travelers staying in the Hotel Zone rarely need to visit downtown unless they're taking an ADO bus to Playa del Carmen or Chichen Itza.
How far is Cancún airport from the Hotel Zone? +
CUN Airport is approximately 10 miles from the southern end of the Hotel Zone and 20–22 miles from the northern hotel clusters near the convention center. Transfer time is typically 20–40 minutes under normal traffic conditions. Official licensed taxi fares from CUN to the Hotel Zone are posted at fixed-rate kiosks in the arrivals hall — fares are fixed by zone, so verify the current posted rate on arrival; as an illustrative planning figure, Hotel Zone transfers have typically run approximately $35–55 USD. Pre-booked shared shuttles are generally cheaper for groups. Use the official fixed-rate taxi kiosk inside the terminal before exiting — do not accept transportation offers from people who approach you on the arrivals floor.
Is all-inclusive worth it in Cancún? +
For most visitors, yes. All-inclusive packages in Cancún's Hotel Zone are well-established and competitively priced compared to paying independently for a resort hotel, three meals per day, and drinks. The Hotel Zone has limited independent restaurant options in the mid-range, and taxi costs between properties add up quickly. All-inclusive is especially good value for families and for travelers who plan to spend most of their time on the resort beach. Independent travelers who want to explore Playa del Carmen or local restaurants may find that an independent hotel in Playa del Carmen is better suited to their goals.
When is the best time to visit Cancún? +
December through April is the best window — dry, sunny, and outside the Atlantic hurricane season. January and February offer the most reliably pleasant weather with fewer crowds and no spring break surge. March and early April coincide with US spring break, which significantly increases crowds and pricing. November is a good shoulder month with lower prices and calmer weather. The Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30, with statistical peak around September 10. Sargassum seaweed affects some Hotel Zone beaches most heavily from April through September. Source: NOAA National Hurricane Center.
Is Cancún safe for tourists in 2026? +
Quintana Roo (which includes Cancún) is rated Level 2 — Exercise Increased Caution by the US State Department (May 2026). Travelers in the Hotel Zone most frequently encounter timeshare solicitation at the airport and on the strip, and petty theft is a common concern in crowded market and beach areas. Follow standard precautions: secure valuables, use official transportation, and review the current US State Department advisory for the most recent conditions. Use official licensed transportation from the airport and book excursions through the resort or reputable operators. Exercise additional caution in areas away from the main tourist corridor. Source: US State Department — Mexico Travel Advisory, May 2026.
