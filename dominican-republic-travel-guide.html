Dominican Republic Travel Guide

Punta Cana, Puerto Plata, Santo Domingo — which DR destination fits your trip? Plus entry requirements, best time to visit, and what to budget.

Updated November 2026 • Caribbean • Complete Planning Hub

Where to Go Best Time Getting There Budget Couples & Families Entry Requirements Safety Planning Toolkit FAQs

The Dominican Republic is the most visited destination in the Caribbean — and not by accident. It offers the region's widest range of resort infrastructure, an international airport serving more direct routes from the US than almost anywhere in the Caribbean, and three destinations that appeal to genuinely different types of travelers: Punta Cana for beach and resort vacations, Puerto Plata for culture and adventure, and Santo Domingo for Caribbean history unlike anywhere else in the hemisphere.

This guide helps you decide which of those destinations fits your trip and what you need to know before you book. For detailed Punta Cana planning — resort zones, beach comparisons, transportation — the Punta Cana travel guide goes deeper on the specifics.

Quick answer

For most first-time DR visitors: book Punta Cana. It has the most direct flights, the largest all-inclusive resort selection, and a beach corridor that is easy to navigate for first-timers. Puerto Plata suits travelers who want a culturally richer, less resort-packaged experience. Santo Domingo is best added to any trip where Caribbean history and architecture are a priority. All travelers must complete the free E-Ticket form before arriving and departing — not just on arrival.

The Dominican Republic holds a crowd score of 6 and budget pressure score of 5 on the Tourism Demand Index — one of the Caribbean's strongest value propositions for all-inclusive travelers. See how it compares to 29 other destinations.

These three destinations are within the same country but serve meaningfully different types of trips. The comparison below covers the key practical differences that affect most travelers' decisions.

Punta Cana

Punta Cana and the adjacent Bávaro strip are where the Dominican Republic's resort industry is most concentrated. The area spans roughly 50 kilometers of coastline along the country's eastern tip, with calm, shallow water protected by offshore reefs — genuinely excellent beach conditions for families and casual swimmers.

Most visitors stay entirely within their resort, and the infrastructure makes this easy: the PUJ airport is 25 minutes from the main hotel zone, resort transfers are included in most all-inclusive packages, and the beaches are well-maintained (including daily sargassum removal during the season). For travelers who want a simple, predictable beach vacation with minimal logistics, Punta Cana delivers that reliably. Read the full Punta Cana guide for resort zone breakdowns, planning advice, and what to know before you book.

Puerto Plata

Puerto Plata occupies the north Atlantic coast and has a genuinely different character from Punta Cana. The city itself has Victorian-era gingerbread architecture from its 19th-century tobacco wealth, and the surrounding coast includes both resort beaches (Playa Dorada) and world-class kite-surfing territory at Cabarete, which has hosted multiple world championships. The Pico Isabel de Torres cable car climbs to 793 meters for panoramic views and a small botanical garden at the top.

The POP airport is smaller than PUJ, with more limited direct US connections — primarily from Miami and seasonal service from Toronto and Montreal. Travelers considering Puerto Plata from the US East Coast should verify available direct routes before booking; a connection through Miami or Santo Domingo is sometimes required.

Santo Domingo

Santo Domingo's Ciudad Colonial is the oldest continuously inhabited European settlement in the Americas, containing the first cathedral, first university, and first permanent European street built in the New World. It was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1990. The Colonial Zone is compact enough to walk in a day, but it rewards slower exploration — the architecture is genuinely extraordinary by Caribbean standards.

As a base for a beach vacation, Santo Domingo is the wrong choice — the nearest beach (Boca Chica) is a 30-minute drive and is primarily used by locals. As a cultural and historical destination, or as a two- to three-night addition to a longer DR itinerary, it is worth the visit.

Best Time to Visit the Dominican Republic

The DR's climate is tropical, with consistent warmth year-round and two meaningful seasons. The timing of your trip matters primarily for hurricane risk, rainfall, and pricing — not because any month is unpleasantly cold.

Best Season
December – April
Dry season. Consistent sunshine, low humidity, calm Caribbean water. Peak prices and demand.
Shoulder Season
May & November
Some rain, mostly fine weather. 20–35% cheaper than peak season. Good value window.
Hurricane Season Peak
August – October
Highest storm risk. The Atlantic peak runs through September. Travel insurance is essential.

January through March is the premium travel window. Rainfall across the Punta Cana coast averages under two inches per month, sea temperatures hover around 78–80°F (25–27°C), and the trade winds keep afternoons comfortable even at high resort prices. This is when the Dominican Republic is most crowded and most expensive.

November is consistently underrated. After early November the island quiets down, resort rates drop meaningfully, and most years weather holds well before the dry season fully sets in. Flexible travelers who can book in November often find the best value-to-weather ratio of the year.

Hurricane season: The Atlantic hurricane season officially runs June 1 through November 30 (NOAA), with peak activity statistically concentrated around September 10. The DR has been affected by major storms — most recently Hurricane Maria in 2017. Any trip during August, September, or October should include comprehensive travel insurance with trip cancellation and interruption coverage. Source: NHC Tropical Cyclone Climatology, verified November 2026.

Getting to the Dominican Republic

The DR has three main international airports. Which one you use depends almost entirely on where in the country you are going.

Punta Cana International Airport (PUJ)

PUJ is the busiest airport in the Dominican Republic and one of the busiest in the Caribbean — it handles the vast majority of resort-bound tourist traffic. It is privately operated, which means it is better maintained and more efficiently run than many regional airports. Direct service operates from most major US hubs including New York (JFK, EWR), Miami, Atlanta, Philadelphia, Charlotte, Boston, Chicago, and multiple other cities. Airlines with consistent Punta Cana service include American, Delta, United, JetBlue, Southwest, and Frontier. From the US East Coast, flight times run 3.5 to 4 hours. Air Canada, WestJet, and Air Transat serve the route from Toronto and Montreal.

Las Américas International Airport (SDQ) — Santo Domingo

SDQ is the country's second-busiest airport and the right choice for travelers arriving in the capital. It connects directly with Miami, New York, Toronto, and multiple Latin American hubs. For Punta Cana-focused trips, SDQ adds 2.5 to 3 hours of overland travel and is not a practical alternative to PUJ unless flight pricing or availability makes it unavoidable.

Gregorio Luperón International Airport (POP) — Puerto Plata

POP primarily handles charter and seasonal service, with the most consistent connections from Toronto, Montreal, and Miami. Direct US service is limited — verify available routes before choosing Puerto Plata as a destination if you are traveling from outside the Northeast. Travelers from most US cities will find it easier to fly into PUJ and transfer overland to the north coast if necessary, though this adds 4+ hours of driving.

Airport to resort transfers

  • Resort shuttle (included): Nearly all Punta Cana all-inclusives include airport pickup. Confirm at booking and find your transfer representative in the PUJ arrivals hall.
  • Private transfers: Multiple DR-based companies offer fixed-rate private transfers from PUJ. Rates vary; agree on a price in advance and use a licensed operator.
  • Official airport taxis: Available at clearly marked taxi stands outside the terminal. Agree on the fare before departing.

Budget Planning

The Dominican Republic is one of the Caribbean's best-value destinations, particularly for all-inclusive travelers. The country's large inventory of competing resorts keeps prices lower than comparable Caribbean all-inclusives in the US Virgin Islands or the Turks and Caicos. That said, your actual daily spend depends significantly on how you choose to travel.

Couples and Families in the Dominican Republic

For couples

Punta Cana has a strong adults-only all-inclusive offering. The Excellence Collection (Excellence Punta Cana and Cap Cana) operates couples-focused adults-only properties with high service levels and refined amenities. Zoëtry Agua Punta Cana offers a smaller boutique adults-only experience, and the Breathless Punta Cana Resort is popular for a younger couples' demographic. For travelers who want something less resort-packaged, the Cap Cana development south of PUJ includes boutique hotel options alongside the larger properties. Puerto Plata's boutique hotels and the quieter beach scene at Cabarete can also suit couples looking for a more locally authentic DR experience.

For families

Punta Cana's large-scale all-inclusives are well-suited for families. Iberostar Bávaro Suites (family-friendly, with confirmed kids' club — not to be confused with the adults-only Iberostar Grand Bávaro), Barceló Bávaro Palace, Riu Bambu (family-friendly with water park), and Hard Rock Hotel & Casino Punta Cana all offer family-specific programming, kids' clubs, and water park facilities within the resort. Dreams Punta Cana and Memories Splash are designed around family-first layouts. One practical note: the beach near many Punta Cana resorts has gentle surf in calm periods but can develop stronger waves during weather systems — confirm current beach conditions with your resort on arrival, particularly if you have young children. Calm-water resort pools are always available as an alternative.

Entry Requirements for US Citizens

Verify before you travel: Entry requirements can change. The information below is based on sources verified in November 2026. Check the US State Department and the Dominican Republic Directorate General of Migration (DGM) for the most current requirements before your trip.

Visa

US citizens do not need a visa to visit the Dominican Republic for tourism. No advance visa application is required.

Passport validity

The Dominican Republic's Directorate General of Migration (DGM) announced a documented temporary exception to passport validity requirements, effective December 1, 2025 through December 31, 2026. Under this exception, US citizens (among other qualifying nationalities) are not required to have six months of remaining passport validity beyond their entry date. Instead, the passport must remain valid throughout the duration of the stay and through the departure date — it cannot expire while you are in the country.

Important qualifications:

  • The US State Department still lists a six-month validity recommendation in its general guidance for the DR — this reflects the standard rule, not the temporary DGM exception.
  • Airlines maintain their own boarding policies independently of immigration rules. Your airline may still require six months of passport validity regardless of the DGM exception. Confirm with your airline before booking if your passport is within six months of expiry.
  • Travelers should verify the current status of this exception with the DGM (migracion.gob.do) or their airline before booking, as conditions may change.

Tourism fee ($10 USD)

A $10 USD tourism fee applies to all air arrivals. For flights booked outside the Dominican Republic, this fee is bundled automatically into the airline ticket price — no separate payment at the airport is required. At land and sea ports of entry, the $10 fee is still paid separately.

E-Ticket — mandatory for all travelers

The E-Ticket is required for both arrival AND departure. You must complete a separate form each time. Many travelers are unaware that a departure form is also required — missing it can cause delays at the airport before your flight home.

The E-Ticket (eticket.migracion.gob.do) is a free digital form combining your immigration declaration, customs declaration, and health declaration into a single QR code submission. It replaces the paper forms previously filled out on the aircraft. When completed, you receive a QR code to show at check-in and at the immigration booth.

You will need to provide: your full legal name as it appears on your passport, passport number, flight number and airline, the address of your first night's accommodation in the DR, a customs declaration of your luggage contents and any cash above $10,000 USD, and a contact email address. The form can be completed any time before you travel and is recommended before leaving for the airport rather than on-site.

Source: DGM E-Ticket service page, verified November 2026.

Safety and Travel Preparation

US State Department advisory: The Dominican Republic is rated Level 2 — Exercise Increased Caution as of June 2025, citing crime including robbery, homicide, and sexual assault. The majority of incidents affect specific urban areas rather than the resort zones. Source: US State Department, verified November 2026.

The major resort zones in Punta Cana and Puerto Plata have significant security presence. The US State Department recommends standard precautions regardless of your destination:

  • Use resort-arranged or hotel-recommended transportation rather than informal drivers
  • Avoid displaying expensive jewelry, electronics, or large amounts of cash
  • Stay in well-lit commercial areas after dark; ask your resort about which areas are appropriate to explore independently
  • Keep a photocopy or photo of your passport stored separately from the original
  • Have a list of your resort's address and emergency contacts saved on your phone

The State Department also notes that most private hospitals in the DR require upfront payment and do not directly bill US health insurance. Travel insurance that includes medical coverage and emergency evacuation is strongly recommended — particularly important for the DR given this payment structure.

Frequently Asked Questions About the Dominican Republic

Do US citizens need a visa to visit the Dominican Republic? +
No. US citizens enter the Dominican Republic visa-free for tourism. A $10 USD tourism fee is bundled into airline ticket prices for flights purchased outside the DR — no separate airport payment is required for air arrivals. All travelers must also complete the free E-Ticket digital entry form at eticket.migracion.gob.do before arrival and again before departure.
What is the Dominican Republic E-Ticket and do I need it? +
The E-Ticket is a mandatory free digital form at eticket.migracion.gob.do that combines immigration, customs, and health declarations into a single QR code. All travelers entering or departing the Dominican Republic are required to complete it — once for arrival and again for departure. It replaces the paper forms previously handed out on aircraft. Complete it before leaving for the airport to avoid delays. It is free of charge.
Is Punta Cana or Puerto Plata better for a first visit? +
Punta Cana is the better choice for most first-time visitors who want a beach-focused all-inclusive vacation. It has the most US direct flights, the widest resort selection, and the most straightforward airport logistics. Puerto Plata suits travelers who want a more culturally varied experience — colonial architecture, north-coast beaches, kite surfing at Cabarete, and a less resort-packaged atmosphere. Both are worth considering; the distinction comes down to whether your priority is resort simplicity or cultural variety.
When is the best time to visit the Dominican Republic? +
December through April is the best window — the dry season brings consistent sunshine, low humidity, and calm Caribbean water. January through March is the most reliable stretch. May and November offer good weather at lower prices (shoulder season). The Atlantic hurricane season runs June through November, with peak risk in August, September, and early October — travel insurance is strongly advised during those months.
Is the Dominican Republic safe for tourists? +
The US State Department rates the Dominican Republic at Level 2 — Exercise Increased Caution (June 2025), citing crime including robbery, homicide, and sexual assault. The major resort corridors in Punta Cana and Puerto Plata have significant security presence. The US State Department recommends standard precautions: use resort or licensed transportation, avoid displaying valuables, stay in well-lit commercial areas after dark, follow guidance from your resort, and book excursions through reputable operators. Review the current advisory at travel.state.gov before travel. Travel insurance with medical coverage is strongly recommended — most private hospitals in the DR require upfront payment and do not bill US insurance directly.
